# Abuse procedure

Status: draft template. This is not legal advice. Reporting duties, retention rules and safe-handling rules differ by country. Adapt this procedure with legal advice before you use it for a public or team instance.

## Aims

1. Stop the gateway being used to reach illegal or harmful material.
2. Never copy, store, forward or look at such material more than the law and your duty require.
3. Report to the right authority.
4. Keep the operator and the people reviewing reports safe.

## Triggers

- The name check flags an address or a page title.
- A visitor uses the "report this site" button. The report file holds the time, the address, one reason from a fixed list and the reporter's subject, and nothing from the page.
- A third party sends a takedown or abuse complaint.
- An operator or reviewer comes across something while working.

## Steps

1. **Block first.** Add the onion to the blocklist. This stops everyone from opening it, and takes effect without waiting for review. A site that the name check or enough visitor reports has already put on the held list is not served either, and its line in the held file is the record of when and why.
2. **Do not view more than you must.** The default viewer withholds images, so a reviewer normally sees a placeholder, not the image. Do not reveal images from a flagged site to confirm it. Do not download files from it.
3. **Do not copy or forward the material.** That includes screenshots, saved pages and evidence captures. Delete any capture that already holds it, according to the law that applies to you, and record that you did.
4. **Record only what is needed.** Keep the onion address, the time, who reported it, what triggered the flag, and what action was taken. Do not record the content.
5. **Report.** Send the address, not the content, to the competent authority or hotline for your country. Many countries have a national hotline for child sexual abuse material, and a network of hotlines is listed at inhope.org. For other illegal content, use the route your law provides.
6. **Review the account.** If a signed-in visitor opened the material, apply your account policy. Repeated hits should suspend viewer access pending review.
7. **Close the report.** Tell the reporter the outcome if you can, and note it in the log.

## Safety of reviewers

Reviewers can be harmed by what they see. Rotate reviewers, limit exposure, give a way to step away, and offer support. The aim of the software is that nobody has to see the material at all.

## Things to decide before launch

- Who the abuse contact is and how they are reached.
- Which authority or hotline you report to, and who is allowed to make the report.
- How long you keep the minimal records in step 4.
- Who can unblock an onion, and on what evidence.
- Whether and how you answer takedown complaints.
